# R11 — Classical background and limits of the literature check

Consulted 21 September 2026, Europe/Istanbul. The proof in PROOF.md is
self-contained. These sources identify established tools and prevent
an unsupported novelty claim. This is a targeted background check,
not an exhaustive priority search for the quantitative theta result.

1. Alexei Shadrin, *Approximation Theory*, Cambridge Part III lecture
   notes, chapter 4, especially the L1-approximation and Hobby–Rice
   material in sections 4.3–4.4.
   [Author's course PDF](https://www.damtp.cam.ac.uk/user/na/PartIIIat/b04.pdf).
   The search-indexed excerpt describes the sign-annihilator condition
   for L1 approximation and the Hobby–Rice theorem. Direct PDF retrieval
   repeatedly timed out; the complete PDF was not reviewed. Used only
   as background for the standard mechanism, not as the sole basis of
   any R11 proof step.

2. Oleg Lazarev and Elliott H. Lieb, *A smooth, complex generalization
   of the Hobby–Rice theorem*, Indiana University Mathematics Journal
   62 (2013), 1133–1141. DOI: 10.1512/iumj.2013.62.5062.
   [Author-submitted abstract and journal reference](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5059),
   [publisher record](https://iumj.org/article/6000/).
   The arXiv abstract and journal metadata were directly read. The
   publisher record was found in search; a later direct fetch failed
   with a gateway error. The abstract describes a smooth **complex
   unit-modulus** annihilating multiplier. That is not a positive real
   kernel multiplier, and its theorem is not imported as a proof of
   our positivity result. It establishes relevant historical context
   for moment annihilation and smoothness issues.

3. Charles R. Hobby and John R. Rice, *A moment problem in L1
   approximation*, Proceedings of the American Mathematical Society
   16 (1965), 665–670. DOI: 10.1090/S0002-9939-1965-0178292-5.
   [AMS bibliographic record](https://www.ams.org/journals/proc/1965-016-04/S0002-9939-1965-0178292-5/).
   Original full text was not retrieved. Historical attribution is
   supported by the course notes and Lazarev–Lieb abstract; no claim
   is made that the original paper was fully inspected.

R11 does not claim to invent finite-dimensional moment constraints,
Gram-matrix right inverses, L1 best approximation, L-infinity moment
duality, smooth correction, or standard order-four unfolding geometry.
The specific cost question, exact-Q constraints and certified numerical
bracket are the present research content. Establishing the novelty and
importance of a future sharper quantitative theorem remains work.

These sources concern mathematical tools. They do not supply evidence
for physical stability, for a dBN bound, for the Riemann hypothesis, or
for a global statement about the original theta transform's zeros.
